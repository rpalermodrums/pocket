# What’s Pocket?

## The Arby's fixins bar

I spent the early part of my adult life playing music for a living. All my mentors told me some version of the same thing, but Conrad Herwig put it best: "If you can't imagine living off the fixins bar at Arby's every day, this life isn't for you." Every so often he'd go off about getting chased out of one for stealing the bread 'n' butter pickles.

I believed him completely. I was also pretty sure I'd be happy at the fixins bar.

Then the money ran out. So I begrudgingly learned to code, mostly because I'd heard it paid well and figured I was smart enough. I expected software to be rote and unfulfilling, the opposite of music. It turned out I liked it quite a lot, and the pain of selling out never came.

Still, music matters to me in a way software never will. I owe a lot to the people I grew up playing with and learning from, and for a while now I've wanted to put these new skills to work for them.

## Trane: a Living Band in a Box

For years that meant a project called Trane, after Coltrane. I wanted a band in a box that could listen and respond to what you played. Backing tracks are useful, but if you try something weird, the backing track doesn't care. I wanted something to practice with that would care, in whatever sense a computer can. Something that might give me a reason to try something different the next time through.

I got far enough to see how much work that would be: analysis, source separation, transcription, accompaniment, live input. Latency was a bitch. Every part seemed to need several other parts finished first.

## Some Things to Reconsider

I grew up playing jazz, and I gave electronic music the snob treatment for reasons I can't defend now. That changed one night in Berlin, when some new friends and a pretty girl dragged me from my hostel in Kotti to Watergate for Robag Wruhme's all-nighter.

Drink in hand, walking toward the dancefloor, I got swept up in a Senegalese djembe pattern in 7/8 laid over a four-on-the-floor kick. Half the students in my conservatory program would have struggled to clap 1-2-3-4 over something in 7/8. Nobody on that dancefloor seemed bothered. Up in the booth, Wruhme had set his beer down on the turntable. He lifted the pint each time just before the needle came around, then set it gently back down.

I had some things to reconsider.

Years later I showed up to an open decks night at Bossa Nova Civic Club without having learned the controls. I'd also forgotten my USB, so I went in blind with someone else's record bag. It didn't go well. But some kind people showed me how to actually use the decks, invited me to practice after hours at a local record shop, and told me to come back.

I was humbled, and I learned a lot, quickly.

Mostly I learned how little it helps to think about a track on its own. I could love it and still have no business playing it right then. What came before matters. So does what I'm going to do next, and whether anyone in the room is interested. Last Tuesday's good idea might be terrible tonight. There are people out there with incredible ears, and some of them found their way into this. The medium has so much potential, and I want to pour gasoline on that fire.

## Deconstructing Trane, Creating Pocket

That's why I built Pocket. I'd been sitting on all this work from Trane, and something I was doing with agents finally made me think I could use it without finishing the whole project first: break it into a bunch of small tools for working with recordings, MIDI, and arrangements, give those to an agent, and see what happens. The agent already knows how to use a computer. I don't need to build another app just so it can find the bit of audio I'm talking about.

I've been using Pocket with Ableton Live to build sets. I give the agent some records and an idea of where I want to go, listen to what comes back, and tell it what I liked and what bothered me. Then I do it again, sometimes to an unreasonable degree. It's been a good way to find out what I actually wanted. A couple of those sets I keep putting on afterward, which is encouraging given how sick of them I should be by now.

I still want to build Trane. I want to practice with something that can follow me when I go somewhere, and occasionally take me somewhere I wouldn't have gone on my own. I don't know how much of that I'll get right, but I can build useful pieces of it now and let other people play with them. Someone else's idea of what to do with this will probably be more interesting than mine anyway.

Come sit in.

Much more to come,<br>
