## What this agreement does, in brief

This summary is not part of the agreement.

- **You keep your copyright.** This is a license, not an assignment. You can use
  your own contribution in any other way you like.
- **You let the maintainer license your contribution broadly.** That includes
  Pocket's open source license and, for people building closed products on the
  core, a separate commercial license. Commercial licenses fund Pocket's
  maintenance so the core can stay free. [Licensing](LICENSING.md) explains
  the model.
- **Your contribution stays open source.** Section 4 promises that anything you
  contribute will remain available to everyone under an open source license
  approved by the Open Source Initiative.
- **You confirm the work is yours to give** and tell the project about any
  third-party code or restrictions you know of.

## How to sign

When this agreement is in effect, a bot will ask first-time contributors to
sign by posting this comment on their pull request:

```text
I have read the Pocket Contributor License Agreement and I hereby sign it.
```

The bot records your GitHub username, the pull request and the date. Signing
once covers your future contributions. The CLA isn't in effect yet; see
[Contributing](CONTRIBUTING.md#contributor-license-agreement) for the current
status.

## Differences from the Apache ICLA

Listed for review. This section is not part of the agreement.

- "The Foundation" (the Apache Software Foundation) is replaced by "the
  Maintainer" throughout, and the Work is Pocket.
- The preamble's commitment that the Foundation won't use Contributions contrary
  to its nonprofit status is removed. It's replaced by the open source
  commitment in Section 4, which is new.
- Section 2 adds a sentence stating that the Maintainer may license
  Contributions under terms of the Maintainer's choosing, including commercial
  licenses, subject to Section 4. It grants a license; it does not assign
  copyright.
- Section 5 omits the Apache reference to a separate Corporate CLA.
- Section 10 (assignment to a successor) is new.
- The name, address and signature fields of the Apache form are replaced by a
  pull-request comment recorded by a bot.
- Section headings are added and some sections are renumbered. The wording of
  the patent grant, representations, disclaimer and third-party submission
  sections follows the Apache text.

## Questions for legal review

Listed for review. This section is not part of the agreement.

- Should the counterparty be Ryan Palermo personally, or a legal entity such as
  an LLC or a fiscal sponsor? How should succession (Section 10) work?
- Does Section 4 achieve its goal, that every contribution always stays
  available under an OSI-approved license, without preventing commercial
  licenses of the same code? It's modeled on the outbound-license approach of
  the Project Harmony agreements.
- Is a corporate CLA needed for contributors whose employers own their work?
- Which governing law and venue should apply? The Apache text specifies
  neither.
- Should the agreement ask contributors to disclose substantial use of AI tools
  in a contribution?
